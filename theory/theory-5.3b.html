Phase 5.3b: Parametric Non-Linear Pullback & Hyper-reduction

Green-Lagrange Strains, Tangent Stiffness Operators, and Non-Linear ECSW Calibration

Step 1: Kinematic Non-Linearity & Pullbacks

To simulate large-deflection dynamics of notched structures, Phase 5.3b introduces geometric non-linearity. Displacements are modeled using the non-linear Green-Lagrange strain tensor $\mathbf{E}$, which maps strains to the reference configuration:

$$\mathbf{E} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{F} - \mathbf{I}\right)$$

In Voigt notation for a bivariate plane stress element, the Green-Lagrange strain components pulled back to physical coordinates are:

$$\begin{aligned} E_{xx} &= \frac{\partial u_x}{\partial x} + \frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{\partial u_x}{\partial x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial u_y}{\partial x}\right)^2\right] \\ E_{yy} &= \frac{\partial u_y}{\partial y} + \frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{\partial u_x}{\partial y}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial u_y}{\partial y}\right)^2\right] \\ 2E_{xy} &= \left(\frac{\partial u_x}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial u_y}{\partial x}\right) + \left(\frac{\partial u_x}{\partial x}\frac{\partial u_x}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial u_y}{\partial x}\frac{\partial u_y}{\partial y}\right) \end{aligned}$$

The corresponding energetically conjugate stress is the **Second Piola-Kirchhoff (PK2) stress tensor** $\mathbf{S}$, which maintains a linear constitutive relation with $\mathbf{E}$:

$$\mathbf{S} = \mathbf{D} \mathbf{E}$$

Non-Linear Strain-Displacement Relation

The virtual strain increments relate to displacement increments via the non-linear strain-displacement matrix: $$\mathbf{B}_L(\mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{B}_0 + \mathbf{B}_{NL}(\mathbf{u})$$ where $\mathbf{B}_0$ represents the standard linear shape derivatives matrix, and $\mathbf{B}_{NL}(\mathbf{u})$ scales linearly with current displacement gradients, providing the stress-stiffening coupling terms.

Step 2: Tangent Stiffness & Residual Assembly

In non-linear mechanics, solving the equilibrium equations requires calculating the internal force vector $\mathbf{f}_{\text{int}}(\mathbf{u})$ and the tangent stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}_T(\mathbf{u})$ at each iterative state.

The internal force vector evaluates the virtual work of PK2 stresses:

$$\mathbf{f}_{\text{int}}(\mathbf{u}) = \int_{\hat{\Omega}} \mathbf{B}_L^{\mathrm{T}}(\mathbf{u}) \mathbf{S} \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) \det(\mathbf{J}_{\text{ref}}) \, \mathrm{d}\hat{\Omega}$$

The tangent stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}_T(\mathbf{u})$ is split into material and geometric contributions:

$$\mathbf{K}_T(\mathbf{u}) = \mathbf{K}_M(\mathbf{u}) + \mathbf{K}_G(\mathbf{u})$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{K}_M(\mathbf{u}) = \int \mathbf{B}_L^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{D} \mathbf{B}_L \, d\Omega$ is the material tangent stiffness matrix representing strain changes.
• $\mathbf{K}_G(\mathbf{u}) = \int \mathbf{G}_L^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{M}_{\text{stress}} \mathbf{G}_L \, d\Omega$ is the geometric (initial stress) stiffness matrix containing stress terms $S_{xx}, S_{yy}, S_{xy}$, representing local stiffening under load.

Single-Pass Unified Assembly

To optimize runtime evaluation, assembleMappedNonlinearForceAndStiffness evaluates the strains, stresses, B-matrices, internal forces, and both stiffness matrices in a single quadrature loop over the reference coordinates, cutting integration overhead in half.

Step 3: Newton-Raphson Transient ROM Solver

Applying the Newmark-$\beta$ method to non-linear dynamic equations leads to an iterative optimization problem at each timestep:

$$\mathbf{M} \mathbf{a}_{n+1}^{(i)} + \mathbf{C} \mathbf{v}_{n+1}^{(i)} + \mathbf{f}_{\text{int}}(\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{(i)}) = \mathbf{f}_{\text{ext}}(t_{n+1})$$

We resolve this using a Newton-Raphson loop that updates displacement increments iteratively:

$$\mathbf{K}_{\text{eff}}^{(i)} \mathrm{d}\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{R}^{(i)}, \quad \mathbf{u}^{(i+1)} = \mathbf{u}^{(i)} + \mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{K}_{\text{eff}}^{(i)} = \mathbf{K}_T(\mathbf{u}^{(i)}) \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\gamma \beta_K}{\beta \Delta t}\right) + \mathbf{M} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\beta \Delta t^2} + \frac{\gamma \alpha_M}{\beta \Delta t}\right)$ is the effective tangent operator.
• $\mathbf{R}^{(i)} = \mathbf{f}_{\text{ext}} - \mathbf{f}_{\text{int}}(\mathbf{u}^{(i)}) - \mathbf{M}\mathbf{a}^{(i)} - \mathbf{C}\mathbf{v}^{(i)}$ is the residual forces vector.

Dynamic n-DOF Reduced Coordinate Solver

Unlike linear ROMs that can solve low-DOF systems analytically using Cramer's rule, non-linear dynamics with multi-mode snapshots requires general coordinate handling. Phase 5.3b implements a general $n$-DOF Gaussian elimination solver in reduced coordinate space, resolving arbitrary modal dimensions $\boldsymbol{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times n}$ dynamically (e.g. Mode 1 bending, Mode 2 tension, and Mode 3 mid-span compliance).

Step 4: ECSW ROM Optimization & Calibration

To avoid evaluating stiffnesses and force vectors over the entire mesh at each Newton iteration, Phase 5.3b implements **ECSW hyper-reduction** on the non-linear operators:

$$\mathbf{f}_{r}^{\text{int}}(\mathbf{q}) \approx \sum_{e \in \widetilde{E}} w_e \boldsymbol{\Phi}_e^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{f}_{\text{int},e}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_e\mathbf{q})$$

Virtual Force Fitting & OMP Training

To train the sparse element weights, virtual force signatures are evaluated using the true non-linear internal forces of compliance snapshots:

ECSW Non-Linear Signature Training
1. Project Force Signatures: Evaluate element forces $\mathbf{F}_{e}^{\text{int}}(\mathbf{u}^{(s)})$ and project them to the reduced coordinates space:
    $G_{e, s, m} = \sum_{i=1}^N \Phi_m[i] F_{e, i}^{\text{int}}(\mathbf{u}^{(s)})$
2. Construct NNLS target: $\mathbf{b} = \sum_e \mathbf{G}_e$
3. Run Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (OMP) to select $k$ active elements (up to $k=12$).
4. Assign Calibrated Weights: Store OMP-fitted weights $\mathbf{w}$ directly.

Operating Point Stiffness Calibration

To guarantee natural frequency alignment during parametric notch sweeps, the sparse weights are dynamically scaled at the operating configuration:

$$\gamma = \frac{\boldsymbol{\phi}_1^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{K}_{\text{Full}}(\mu, r) \boldsymbol{\phi}_1}{\boldsymbol{\phi}_1^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{K}_{\text{ECSW}}(\mu, r) \boldsymbol{\phi}_1}, \quad w_e \leftarrow \gamma \cdot w_e$$
