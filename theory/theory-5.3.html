Phase 5.3: The Reference Configuration Mapping Paradigm

Mathematical Pullbacks and Hyper-reduction in Shape-Parametric Digital Twins

Step 1: Physical-Domain FOM (The Naive Remeshing Approach)

In standard shape-parametric simulations, the most intuitive approach is to solve the Full Order Model (FOM) equations of motion directly on the morphed, physical geometry $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ for each design parameter vector $\mathbf{\mu}$:

$$\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{\mu})\ddot{\mathbf{u}}(t) + \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{\mu})\dot{\mathbf{u}}(t) + \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})\mathbf{u}(t) = \mathbf{f}_{\text{ext}}(t; \mathbf{\mu})$$

Where the terms are defined as:
• $\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the parameter-dependent global mass matrix containing inertia terms.
• $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the parameter-dependent damping matrix accounting for energy dissipation.
• $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the parameter-dependent stiffness matrix representing structural resistance.
• $\mathbf{u}(t), \dot{\mathbf{u}}(t), \ddot{\mathbf{u}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ are the displacement, velocity (first time derivative $\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}}{\mathrm{d}t}$), and acceleration (second time derivative $\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\mathbf{u}}{\mathrm{d}t^2}$) vectors respectively, representing the degrees of freedom (DOFs) of $N$ nodes/control points.
• $\mathbf{f}_{\text{ext}}(t; \mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is the parameter-dependent external force vector (e.g. loads or boundary pressures) at time $t$.
• $\mathbf{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^p$ is the $p$-dimensional design parameter vector (describing the morphed geometric notch, thickness, or coordinates).

Under this formulation, the global stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ is obtained by re-generating the NURBS patch mesh and integrating shape functions over the physical coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$:

$$K_{ij}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \int_{\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})} \nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) : \mathbf{D} : \nabla R_j(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) \, \mathrm{d}\Omega$$

Where:
• $K_{ij}(\mathbf{\mu})$ represents the entry of the global stiffness matrix at row $i$ and column $j$, indicating the force at control point $i$ due to a unit displacement at control point $j$.
• $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ is the morphed physical-domain geometry (area or volume) dependent on the shape parameter vector $\mathbf{\mu}$.
• $\mathbf{x} = (x, y, z)$ represents the physical spatial coordinate vector inside the domain $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$.
• $\nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \left[\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y}\right]^{\mathrm{T}}$ is the spatial gradient of the $i$-th NURBS shape function $R_i$ with respect to the physical coordinates $\mathbf{x}$.
• $\mathbf{D}$ is the fourth-order elastic constitutive tensor defining the material stress-strain relation (Hooke's law).
• $:$ is the double contraction operator (representing the tensor product contractive inner product, e.g. $\mathbf{A} : \mathbf{B} = \sum_{k,l} A_{kl} B_{kl}$).
• $\mathrm{d}\Omega$ is the differential volume or area element in physical space coordinates.

Workflow: How the Naive Approach Works

The standard/naive parameter sweep workflow operates by re-solving the physical equations directly on each design configuration:

  1. Define Parameter: Select a target geometric shape parameter vector $\mathbf{\mu}$.
  2. Mesh Generation / Morphing: Regenerate or morph the NURBS control points $\mathbf{P}_{i,j}(\mathbf{\mu})$ to reconstruct the physical coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$.
  3. Numerical Integration: Evaluate the physical shape function gradients $\nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$ at all element Gauss quadrature points and assemble the global stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ over the morphed mesh elements.
  4. Forward Solve: Execute a full order solve to obtain the displacement field vector $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu})$.
  5. SVD Basis Extraction: Collect multiple displacement vectors into snapshot matrix $\mathbf{S}$ and run SVD to extract the spatial modes $\mathbf{V}$.

Critical Blind Spots of the Naive Approach:

  • Vector Space Incompatibility (Mathematical SVD Failure): Projection-based Reduced Order Models, such as Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD), operate under the assumption that all displacement snapshots reside in a unified, parameter-independent vector space $\mathbb{R}^N$. If the physical mesh is regenerated at each parameter state $\mathbf{\mu}_k$, the dimension of the state vector becomes parameter-dependent, $N = N(\mathbf{\mu}_k)$, and control point indexing changes. Consequently, the snapshot matrix: $$\mathbf{S} = [\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu}_1), \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu}_2), \dots, \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu}_S)]$$ possesses columns of varying dimensions ($N(\mathbf{\mu}_i) \neq N(\mathbf{\mu}_j)$). This makes the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of $\mathbf{S}$ algebraically impossible. Here, $\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times S}$ is the snapshot matrix containing displacement columns, $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu}_k)$ is the displacement solution vector for the $k$-th parameter sample, and $S$ is the total number of snapshots. Furthermore, any spatial interpolation onto a common grid changes the coordinates of the nodes themselves, destroying the physical meaning of the basis vectors and the correlation between snapshots.
  • Destructive Computational Assembly (Online Complexity $\mathcal{O}(N_{el})$): To evaluate the global stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ online, the domain must be re-discretized. This requires computing parameter-dependent NURBS coordinates and evaluating the shape functions $R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$ and their gradients $\nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$ at the Gauss integration points for all elements: $$K_{ij}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{e=1}^{N_{el}} \int_{\Omega_e(\mathbf{\mu})} \nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) : \mathbf{D} : \nabla R_j(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) \, \mathrm{d}\Omega$$ where $N_{el}$ is the number of elements in the mesh, $\Omega_e(\mathbf{\mu})$ is the physical domain of the $e$-th element, $n_g$ is the number of Gauss points, and $n_{en}$ is the number of local node functions. The online complexity of this process scales as $\mathcal{O}(N_{el} \cdot n_g \cdot n_{en}^2)$. This assembly takes seconds or minutes, completely violating the sub-millisecond response time required for real-time Digital Twins.
  • Loss of Parameter Differentiability (Discontinuous Sensitivities): Many engineering applications rely on gradient-based optimization and parameter sensitivity calculations, which require the existence of derivatives like $\frac{\partial \mathbf{K}}{\partial \mathbf{\mu}}$ and $\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial \mathbf{\mu}}$. Because mesh regeneration is a discrete topological operation, changes in $\mathbf{\mu}$ introduce abrupt jumps in node locations and mesh connectivity. The mapping $\mathbf{\mu} \mapsto \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ is therefore discontinuous: $$\lim_{\delta \mathbf{\mu} \to \mathbf{0}} \left( \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu} + \delta \mathbf{\mu}) - \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu}) \right) \neq \mathbf{0}$$ where $\delta\mathbf{\mu}$ is an infinitesimal parameter change vector. This lack of differentiability introduces severe numerical noise and causes sensitivity calculations to fail or diverge.

Step 2: Pullback Mapping on a Static Reference Domain

To secure algebraic compatibility, we pull back the governing physical equations from the parameter-dependent domain $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ to a permanently static, undeformed reference configuration $\hat{\Omega}$ using a smooth bijective geometric mapping $\mathbf{\Phi}$:

$$\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{\Phi}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}), \quad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega(\mathbf{\mu}), \quad \hat{\mathbf{x}} \in \hat{\Omega}$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{\Phi}$ is the smooth, bijective coordinate morphing mapping (diffeomorphism).
• $\hat{\mathbf{x}} = (\hat{x}, \hat{y}, \hat{z})$ is the coordinate vector in the static reference domain $\hat{\Omega}$.
• $\mathbf{x} = (x, y, z)$ is the mapped coordinate vector in the parameter-dependent physical domain $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$.

The spatial gradients in physical coordinates are pulled back to the reference coordinates via the inverse transpose of the mapping's Jacobian tensor $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{\Phi}}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}}$:

$$\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} R_i(\hat{\mathbf{x}}) = \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \hat{R}_i(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$$

Where:
• $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}$ is the gradient operator with respect to physical coordinates $\mathbf{x}$.
• $\nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ is the gradient operator with respect to reference coordinates $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$.
• $\hat{R}_i(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ is the $i$-th NURBS shape function evaluated in reference space.
• $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}$ is the transpose of the inverse Jacobian matrix of the coordinate mapping $\mathbf{\Phi}$, where the Jacobian matrix is defined as $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{\Phi}}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$ (with $d$ being the spatial dimension).

Transforming the integral boundaries yields the mapped stiffness matrix, computed entirely on the static reference domain $\hat{\Omega}$:

$$K_{ij}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \int_{\hat{\Omega}} \left( \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}} \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \hat{R}_i \right) : \mathbf{D} : \left( \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}} \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \hat{R}_j \right) \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) \, \mathrm{d}\hat{\Omega}$$

Where:
• $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})$ is the determinant of the Jacobian matrix, representing the local volume/area change factor under the coordinate transformation.
• $\mathrm{d}\hat{\Omega}$ is the differential integration element on the static reference configuration domain $\hat{\Omega}$.

Workflow: How Reference Pullback Works

The reference domain pullback avoids regenerating the mesh structure by mapping the equations to a static domain:

  1. Define Reference Domain $\hat{\Omega}$: Build a single, permanently static grid discretization over $\hat{\Omega}$.
  2. Formulate Coordinate Pullback $\mathbf{\Phi}$: Write a smooth mapping $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{\Phi}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu})$ that maps reference coordinates to physical coordinate spaces.
  3. Transform Operators: Evaluate local Jacobian tensors $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}$ and determinants $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})$ to pull back gradients and integration bounds entirely on the static configuration.
  4. Solve Reduced System: Project the global equations onto the Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) basis $\mathbf{V}$ and solve the low-dimensional system for reduced coefficients $\mathbf{a}$.

Estimated Drawbacks (Why Pullback Alone Fails)

1. The "Lifting" Bottleneck ($\mathcal{O}(N_{el})$ Online Scaling): Although mapping to $\hat{\Omega}$ fixes the grid size $N$, the parameter-dependent inverse Jacobian transpose $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_g; \mathbf{\mu})$ and Jacobian determinant $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_g; \mathbf{\mu}))$ appear inside the integral. Consequently, we must re-evaluate these tensors at every single Gauss point $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_g$ of the full mesh online. Even if the system is projected onto a small POD basis $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times r}$ ($r \ll N$), the assembly of the reduced-order stiffness matrix: $$\mathbf{K}_r(\mathbf{\mu}) = \mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu}) \mathbf{V}$$ where $\mathbf{K}_r(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ is the reduced-order stiffness matrix, $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times r}$ is the Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) basis projection matrix containing $r$ spatial modes (with $r \ll N$), and $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the high-dimensional stiffness matrix. The assembly still requires constructing the full $N \times N$ matrix $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ first. The integration complexity remains $\mathcal{O}(N_{el} \cdot n_g \cdot n_{en}^2)$, which scales with the Full Order Model, destroying real-time speeds.

2. Post-processing Coordinate Mapping: The displacement solutions $\mathbf{u}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ are computed in the reference coordinate space and must be pushed forward via $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{\Phi}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu})$ to the physical space for stresses and visualization, adding a spatial transformation overhead.

Key Advantages

1. Unified Mesh Structure: The reference domain is discretized exactly once. No node displacement or mesh regeneration occurs online.

2. Constant Basis Weights: The reference NURBS shape functions $\hat{R}_i(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ and control point weights are static, preserving perfect mathematical exactness.

Offline Precomputability (Connection to Tiso & Rixen):
Because the reference shape functions and their gradients are parameter-independent, they can be pre-evaluated at the Gauss integration points and stored. For affine mappings, we decompose: $$\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{q=1}^Q \theta_q(\mathbf{\mu}) \mathbf{K}_q^{\text{ref}}$$ where $Q$ is the total number of affine expansion terms, $\theta_q(\mathbf{\mu})$ are parameter-dependent scalar coefficients, and $\mathbf{K}_q^{\text{ref}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ are pre-assembled parameter-independent stiffness matrices, allowing $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ to be assembled online in microseconds as a simple linear combination of pre-assembled reference matrices. This mirrors the seminal work by Tiso & Rixen on structural components with circular shape changes.

Step 3: ECSW on Non-Mapped vs. Mapped Configurations

The Energy-Conserving Sampling and Weighting (ECSW) hyper-reduction algorithm accelerates nonlinear structural dynamics by replacing full-mesh spatial integration with a weighted sum over a sparse subset of elements $E^* \subset E$:

$$\mathbf{k}_r(\mathbf{\mu}) \approx \sum_{e \in E^*} w_e \left( \mathbf{\Phi}_e^T \mathbf{k}_e(\mathbf{\mu}) \mathbf{\Phi}_e \right)$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{k}_r(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$ is the hyper-reduced stiffness matrix.
• $E^*$ is the sparse element subset (active elements) selected during offline training.
• $w_e > 0$ is the optimized positive integration weight for element $e$.
• $\mathbf{\Phi}_e \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{dof, e} \times r}$ is the reduced POD basis matrix restricted to the degrees of freedom associated with element $e$.
• $\mathbf{k}_e(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{dof, e} \times n_{dof, e}}$ is the mapped element stiffness matrix of element $e$.

Workflow: How ECSW Hyper-reduction Works

The Energy-Conserving Sampling and Weighting (ECSW) workflow isolates a tiny subset of elements that represent the structural energy:

  1. Offline Training Assembly: Compute and stack projected element internal forces for all training snapshot states to construct the virtual work system matrix $\mathbf{G}$.
  2. NNLS Optimization: Solve the Non-Negative Least Squares (NNLS) problem to identify a sparse subset of elements $E^*$ and positive integration weights $w_e > 0$ that match the full model's virtual forces.
  3. Online Sparse Assembly: During the parametric sweeps, compute local element stiffnesses and forces *only* for elements in $E^*$. Multiply by the optimized weights $w_e$ to build the reduced system in microseconds, bypassing full-mesh evaluation.

ECSW on Non-Mapped Domain (Why it Fails)

1. Sparse Integration Rule Invalidation: ECSW training identifies a sparse subset of elements $E^*$ and positive weights $w_e$ by minimizing the projection error of the internal forces: $$\min_{\mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}} \sum_{s=1}^S \left\| \sum_{e \in E} w_e \mathbf{V}_e^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{f}_{int, e}(\mathbf{u}_s) - \mathbf{V}^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{f}_{int, full}(\mathbf{u}_s) \right\|_2^2$$ where $\mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}$ represents the non-negativity constraint on element integration weights (ensuring physical and numerical stability), $S$ is the number of training displacement snapshots, $E$ is the set of all elements in the full order mesh, $\mathbf{V}$ is the global Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) basis matrix, $\mathbf{V}_e$ is the POD basis restricted to the DOFs of element $e$, $\mathbf{f}_{int, e}(\mathbf{u}_s)$ is the internal force vector of element $e$ evaluated at snapshot state $\mathbf{u}_s$, $\mathbf{f}_{int, full}(\mathbf{u}_s)$ is the global internal force vector of the full order model at snapshot state $\mathbf{u}_s$, and $\|\cdot\|_2$ is the Euclidean $L_2$ norm. If the physical mesh changes with $\mathbf{\mu}$, the element layout shifts. An element index $e$ that was selected offline points to incorrect physical coordinates for a new parameter $\mathbf{\mu}$, rendering the sparse rule geometrically and structurally invalid.

2. Prohibitive Online Optimization: To resolve this, one must solve the Non-Negative Least Squares (NNLS) problem online for every parameter change. Since the NNLS training matrix $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{\mu})$ has dimension $S \cdot r \times N_{el}$ (where $S$ is the number of training states, $r$ is the reduced dimension, and $N_{el}$ is the number of elements), solving this online takes seconds or minutes, defeating the hyper-reduction speedup.

ECSW on Mapped Domain

By pulling the equations back to the static reference domain, the elements are geometrically fixed. The ECSW training can select a single, static reference element subset $E^*$ that remains structurally optimal across the entire parameter space.

Side Note: Mathematical Retrospective on Phase 5.1 & 5.2 (Direct Physical-Domain Hyper-reduction)

In Phase 5.1 (Geometric POD-Galerkin & basic ECSW) and Phase 5.2 (Geometric Defect ROM), model reduction and hyper-reduction were formulated directly on the parameter-dependent physical domain $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$:

  • Phase 5.1a (Direct Coordinate Morphing): The physical coordinates were defined by NURBS mapping $\mathbf{x}(\mathbf{\xi}; \mathbf{\theta}) = \sum R_{i,j}(\mathbf{\xi})\mathbf{P}_{i,j}(\mathbf{\theta})$ using morphed control points $\mathbf{P}_{i,j}(\mathbf{\theta})$. This direct mapping meant that the snapshot vectors $\mathbf{u}_i$ were defined on varying coordinate spaces $\mathbb{R}^{N(\mathbf{\theta}_i)}$.
  • Phase 5.1b & 5.2 (Direct Physical ECSW): The ECSW weights $w_e$ were optimized directly on these morphed coordinate zones. The element stiffness contributions $\mathbf{K}_e(\mathbf{\mu})$ were assembled by integrating over physical elements $\Omega_e(\mathbf{\mu})$.

The Failure Mechanism under Parameter Sweeps: Because the physical coordinates and shape functions changed with $\mathbf{\mu}$, the element virtual force contributions $\mathbf{A}_{e}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \mathbf{\Phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{K}_e(\mathbf{\mu})\mathbf{\Phi}$ were parameter-dependent. As a result, the global system matrix $\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{\mu})$ was highly dynamic. Sticking to a static set of weights $\mathbf{w}$ trained offline introduces a severe geometric projection error when the shape sweeps to a new state $\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}}$: $$\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}}) = \mathbf{b}(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}}) - \sum_{e \in E^*} w_e \mathbf{A}_e(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}}) \neq \mathbf{0}$$ where $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}})$ is the residual virtual force error vector at a new shape parameter state $\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}}$, $\mathbf{b}(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}}) = \mathbf{\Phi}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{f}_{int, full}(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}})$ is the projected target internal force vector of the full order model, and $\mathbf{A}_e(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}}) = \mathbf{\Phi}_e^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{f}_{int, e}(\mathbf{\mu}_{\text{new}})$ is the projected virtual force vector contribution of element $e$. Because the spatial support $\Omega_e(\mathbf{\mu})$ shifted, the sparse integration rule $E^*$ completely missed stress concentration fields (like the shifting defect notch in 5.2) as they moved, causing solver instability and high numerical errors.

To resolve this, in Phase 5.3, we use the following mathematical formulations to substitute the physical-domain approaches of the previous phases:

  • Static Reference Configuration $\hat{\Omega}$: We integrate over a permanently fixed reference coordinate space $\hat{\Omega}$ instead of the parameter-dependent, continuously morphing physical domain $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ used in Phases 5.1 and 5.2.
  • Bijective Diffeomorphism $\mathbf{\Phi}$: We pull back equations via coordinate mapping $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{\Phi}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu})$ instead of direct, unstructured physical control point coordinates morphing.
  • Jacobian Pullback Tensor $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}$: We map the gradients via $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} R_i = \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}} \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \hat{R}_i$ instead of using parameter-dependent, physical gradients $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$. This cleanly isolates the parametric dependency algebraically.
  • Reference-Mapped ECSW Weights: We train the NNLS system using static reference state virtual forces $\mathbf{f}_{int, e}^{\text{ref}}(\mathbf{u}_s)$ instead of parameter-dependent physical forces $\mathbf{f}_{int, e}(\mathbf{u}_s)$. This yields a single, static reference element subset $E^*$ that remains optimal across all geometric parameter changes.
Algorithm: Reference-Mapped ECSW Training
1. Assemble Snapshots: Collect $S$ training displacement vectors on reference domain.
2. Construct G-Matrix: Assemble the virtual work representation for each reference element $e$:
    $\mathbf{G}_{s,e} = \text{vec}\left( \mathbf{\Phi}_e^T \mathbf{f}_{int,e}^{\text{ref}}(u_s) \right)$
3. Assemble Target: Compute full reference domain force: $\mathbf{b}_s = \text{vec}\left( \mathbf{\Phi}^T \mathbf{f}_{int,full}^{\text{ref}}(u_s) \right)$
4. Solve NNLS: Solve the constrained optimization: $\min_{\mathbf{w} \geq 0} \|\mathbf{G}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{b}\|_2$
    (where $\mathbf{G} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \cdot r \times N_{el}}$ is the global virtual force matrix, $\mathbf{w}$ is the integration weights vector, $\mathbf{b}$ is the stacked target vector, and $\text{vec}(\cdot)$ is the vectorization operator converting a matrix into a column vector).
5. Sparsify: Select the indices $E^* = \{e \in E \mid w_e > 0\}$ and store reference integration weights.

Step 4: Justifying the Mapping on Reference Configuration

To provide a mathematically watertight framework for the Digital Twin, the reference configuration mapping is justified as a strict mathematical requirement through three fundamental pillars:

The Three Pillars of Justification:

  1. Pillar 1: Hilbert Space Homogeneity
    Snapshot-based projection (POD) relies on the mathematical assumption that all displacement states reside in a unified, constant-dimensional Hilbert space: $$\mathbf{u}(t; \mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathcal{H}(\hat{\Omega}) \quad \forall \mathbf{\mu} \in \mathcal{P}$$ where $\mathbf{u}(t; \mathbf{\mu})$ is the displacement vector field, $\mathcal{H}(\hat{\Omega}) = H^1_0(\hat{\Omega})$ is the Sobolev Hilbert space (where functions and their gradients are square-integrable and vanish at the Dirichlet boundaries) defined on the static reference domain, and $\mathcal{P}$ is the parameter space domain. The pullback mapping $\mathbf{\Phi}$ acts as the mathematical coordinate transform that projects varying physical manifolds $\mathcal{H}(\Omega(\mathbf{\mu}))$ onto the unified coordinate domain $\mathcal{H}(\hat{\Omega})$.
  2. Pillar 2: Mathematical Offline/Online Splitting
    By mapping to a reference space, the spatial differential operators ($\nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$) are decoupled from the parameter vector $\mathbf{\mu}$. The parameter is entirely isolated inside the algebraic Jacobian tensor $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\mathbf{\mu})$, ensuring that heavy spatial integrals can be pre-computed offline.
  3. Pillar 3: Mathematical Coercivity & Stability
    Using a smooth, bijective diffeomorphism $\mathbf{\Phi}$ guarantees that the geometric transformation preserves the coercivity and boundedness of the bilinear form, ensuring that the reduced tangent stiffness matrix remains positive-definite and algebraically stable.

Workflow: Mathematical Stability & Lax-Milgram Convergence

The mathematical framework relies on these conditions to guarantee structural convergence and unique solutions:

  1. Smooth Diffeomorphism: The mapping $\mathbf{\Phi}$ behaves as a smooth diffeomorphism, meaning the Jacobian determinant satisfies $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) \geq \delta > 0$ everywhere, guaranteeing that elements do not invert or overlap.
  2. Sobolev Space Coercivity: The bilinear form $a(\hat{u}, \hat{v}; \mathbf{\mu})$ remains coercive, meaning there exists a parameter-dependent constant $\alpha(\mathbf{\mu}) \geq \alpha_0 > 0$ preventing singular structural states.
  3. Lax-Milgram Guarantee: Under these two conditions, the Lax-Milgram theorem guarantees the existence of a unique, stable, and bounded displacement field solution $\mathbf{u} \in \mathcal{H}(\hat{\Omega})$ for any parameter state.

Critical Failure Modes: Loss of Diffeomorphism & Coercivity

  • Jacobian Singularities & Topology Folding ($\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) \leq 0$): The coordinate transformation mapping $\mathbf{\Phi}: \hat{\Omega} \to \Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ must represent a smooth, bijective diffeomorphism. If the shape parameters $\mathbf{\mu}$ are driven beyond physical or mathematical limits (e.g., notch radius or mesh warping parameters are too large), elements fold or overlap. In these cases: $$\exists \hat{\mathbf{x}} \in \hat{\Omega} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu})) \leq 0$$ where $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}))$ is the Jacobian determinant evaluated at reference coordinates $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ and parameter $\mathbf{\mu}$. This causes the inverse Jacobian tensor $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-1}$ to be singular or diverge towards infinity, resulting in numerical division-by-zero errors in the gradient pullback $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} R_i = \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}} \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \hat{R}_i$.
  • Loss of Coercivity ($\alpha(\mathbf{\mu}) \to 0$): For the structural partial differential equations to have a unique, stable, and bounded numerical solution, the mapped bilinear form $a(\hat{v}, \hat{v}; \mathbf{\mu})$ must satisfy coercivity in the Sobolev space $H^1_0(\hat{\Omega})$: $$a(\hat{v}, \hat{v}; \mathbf{\mu}) \geq \alpha(\mathbf{\mu}) \|\hat{v}\|_{H^1(\hat{\Omega})}^2 \quad \forall \hat{v} \in H^1_0(\hat{\Omega}) \quad \text{where } \alpha(\mathbf{\mu}) > 0$$ where $a(\hat{v}, \hat{v}; \mathbf{\mu})$ is the bilinear strain energy form representing virtual work, $\alpha(\mathbf{\mu})$ is the parameter-dependent coercivity stability constant, and $\|\hat{v}\|_{H^1(\hat{\Omega})}$ is the Sobolev $H^1$ norm measuring the energy of the displacement function. If the mapping $\mathbf{\Phi}$ ceases to be a diffeomorphism, the minimum eigenvalue of the mapped elasticity tensor approaches zero, forcing the stability constant $\alpha(\mathbf{\mu}) \to 0$. The mapped stiffness matrix loses positive-definiteness, becoming singular or indefinite, causing the online numerical solver to crash, produce infinite displacements, or output NaN values.

Dynamic Mapping Interface Representation

The mapping coordinates the transformation between reference elements and the physical morphed shape. Under a circular notch shape parameter change:

Reference Coordinates ($\hat{x}, \hat{y}$) Static [0, 1] × [0, 1]
Physical Position ($x, y$) Morphed: $\mathbf{\Phi}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mu)$
Jacobian Determinant $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})$ Parameter-dependent Scale
NURBS Basis Functions Static & Parameter-Independent
