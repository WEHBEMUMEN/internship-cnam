Phase 5.3: The Reference Configuration Mapping Paradigm

Mathematical Pullbacks and Hyper-reduction in Shape-Parametric Digital Twins

Step 1: Physical-Domain FOM (The Naive Remeshing Approach)

In standard shape-parametric simulations, the most intuitive approach is to solve the Full Order Model (FOM) equations of motion directly on the morphed, physical geometry $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ for each design parameter vector $\mathbf{\mu}$:

$$\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{\mu})\ddot{\mathbf{u}}(t) + \mathbf{C}(\mathbf{\mu})\dot{\mathbf{u}}(t) + \mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})\mathbf{u}(t) = \mathbf{f}_{\text{ext}}(t; \mathbf{\mu})$$

Where the terms are defined as:
• $\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the parameter-dependent global mass matrix containing inertia terms.
• $\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the parameter-dependent damping matrix accounting for energy dissipation.
• $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is the parameter-dependent stiffness matrix representing structural resistance.
• $\mathbf{u}(t), \dot{\mathbf{u}}(t), \ddot{\mathbf{u}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ are the displacement, velocity (first time derivative $\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{u}}{\mathrm{d}t}$), and acceleration (second time derivative $\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\mathbf{u}}{\mathrm{d}t^2}$) vectors respectively, representing the degrees of freedom (DOFs) of $N$ nodes/control points.
• $\mathbf{f}_{\text{ext}}(t; \mathbf{\mu}) \in \mathbb{R}^N$ is the parameter-dependent external force vector (e.g. loads or boundary pressures) at time $t$.
• $\mathbf{\mu} \in \mathbb{R}^p$ is the $p$-dimensional design parameter vector (describing the morphed geometric notch, thickness, or coordinates).

Under this formulation, the global stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ is obtained by re-generating the NURBS patch mesh and integrating shape functions over the physical coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$:

$$K_{ij}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \int_{\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})} \nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) : \mathbf{D} : \nabla R_j(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) \, \mathrm{d}\Omega$$

Where:
• $K_{ij}(\mathbf{\mu})$ represents the entry of the global stiffness matrix at row $i$ and column $j$, indicating the force at control point $i$ due to a unit displacement at control point $j$.
• $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ is the morphed physical-domain geometry (area or volume) dependent on the shape parameter vector $\mathbf{\mu}$.
• $\mathbf{x} = (x, y, z)$ represents the physical spatial coordinate vector inside the domain $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$.
• $\nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \left[\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y}\right]^{\mathrm{T}}$ is the spatial gradient of the $i$-th NURBS shape function $R_i$ with respect to the physical coordinates $\mathbf{x}$.
• $\mathbf{D}$ is the fourth-order elastic constitutive tensor defining the material stress-strain relation (Hooke's law).
• $:$ is the double contraction operator (representing the tensor product contractive inner product, e.g. $\mathbf{A} : \mathbf{B} = \sum_{k,l} A_{kl} B_{kl}$).
• $\mathrm{d}\Omega$ is the differential volume or area element in physical space coordinates.

Workflow: How the Naive Approach Works

The standard/naive parameter sweep workflow operates by re-solving the physical equations directly on each design configuration:

  1. Define Parameter: Select a target geometric shape parameter vector $\mathbf{\mu}$.
  2. Mesh Generation / Morphing: Regenerate or morph the NURBS control points $\mathbf{P}_{i,j}(\mathbf{\mu})$ to reconstruct the physical coordinates $\mathbf{x} \in \Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$.
  3. Numerical Integration: Evaluate the physical shape function gradients $\nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$ at all element Gauss quadrature points and assemble the global stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ over the morphed mesh elements.
  4. Forward Solve: Execute a full order solve to obtain the displacement field vector $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu})$.
  5. SVD Basis Extraction: Collect multiple displacement vectors into snapshot matrix $\mathbf{S}$ and run SVD to extract the spatial modes $\mathbf{V}$.

Critical Blind Spots of the Naive Approach:

  • Vector Space Incompatibility & Non-Constant Basis Functions: Projection-based Reduced Order Models, such as Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD), operate under the assumption that all displacement snapshots reside in a unified, parameter-independent vector space $\mathbb{R}^N$. If the physical mesh is regenerated or morphed at each parameter state $\mathbf{\mu}_k$:
    • If $N$ varies ($N(\mathbf{\mu}_i) \neq N(\mathbf{\mu}_j)$): The dimension of the state vector becomes parameter-dependent and control point indexing changes. The snapshot matrix $\mathbf{S} = [\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu}_1), \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{\mu}_2), \dots]$ possesses columns of varying dimensions, making Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) algebraically impossible.
    • If $N$ is fixed ($N(\mathbf{\mu}_i) = N(\mathbf{\mu}_j) = N$): Although SVD becomes mathematically possible, the naive approach still fails fundamentally due to non-constant basis functions. Because the physical mesh coordinates morph, the underlying NURBS shape functions $R_a(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$ change their spatial support and spans in physical space. A POD projection basis $\mathbf{\Phi}$ assumes a static physical space representation. When shape functions continuously shift, projecting snapshots onto a static basis $\mathbf{\Phi}$ loses its spatial meaning, rendering the linear space approximation physically inconsistent.
  • Destructive Computational Assembly (Online Complexity $\mathcal{O}(N_{el})$): To evaluate the global stiffness matrix $\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu})$ online, the domain must be re-discretized. This requires computing parameter-dependent NURBS coordinates and evaluating the shape functions $R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$ and their gradients $\nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu})$ at the Gauss integration points for all elements: $$K_{ij}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{e=1}^{N_{el}} \int_{\Omega_e(\mathbf{\mu})} \nabla R_i(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) : \mathbf{D} : \nabla R_j(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) \, \mathrm{d}\Omega$$ Drawbacks of Naive FOM:

    For every change in the defect shape parameter $\mathbf{\mu}$, the mesh coordinates must be fully reconstructed, the stiffness matrix completely integrated and re-assembled, and an $N \times N$ system of equations solved at each timestep. This process is too slow to achieve interactive framerates.

Step 2: Reference Pullback (Coordinate Mapping & Pullbacks)

The Reference Pullback Paradigm

To avoid regenerating mesh structures, Phase 5.3 maps all physical configurations $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$ to a single, static reference domain $\hat{\Omega}$ using a smooth bijective coordinate mapping $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{\Phi}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu})$:

$$\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{\Phi}}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}}, \quad \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) = 1 - \frac{2d(\hat{x})}{H}$$

By pulling back shape derivatives and integration bounds using the inverse transpose Jacobian $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}$ and determinant $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})$, all spatial integrals are evaluated on the static reference grid: $$K_{kl}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{e=1}^{N_{\text{elem}}} \sum_{g=1}^{n_g} \left( \mathbf{B}_k^{\mathrm{T}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_g; \mathbf{\mu}) \mathbf{D} \mathbf{B}_l(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_g; \mathbf{\mu}) \right) \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) \det(\mathbf{J}_{\text{ref}}) \, w_g \, t$$

Standard Galerkin ROM Projection & Cramer's Analytical Solve

Using a Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) basis $\mathbf{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times n}$ ($n \ll N$), the displacement field is projected into a low-dimensional subspace: $$\mathbf{u}(t) \approx \mathbf{\Phi} \mathbf{q}(t)$$ This yields a highly compact, projected system of equations: $$\mathbf{M}_r \ddot{\mathbf{q}}(t) + \mathbf{C}_r \dot{\mathbf{q}}(t) + \mathbf{K}_r(\mathbf{\mu}) \mathbf{q}(t) = \mathbf{f}_r(t)$$

Speedup Impact: Cramer's Analytical Solve (~30x Solve Speedup)

Because $n = 2$ in our reduced coordinates space, the effective stiffness equations at each integration timestep are solved analytically in $\mathcal{O}(1)$ operations using Cramer's rule: $$\mathrm{d}q_1 = \frac{R_1 K_{22} - R_2 K_{12}}{\det(\mathbf{K}_{\text{eff}, r})}, \quad \mathrm{d}q_2 = \frac{K_{11} R_2 - K_{21} R_1}{\det(\mathbf{K}_{\text{eff}, r})}$$ By completely bypassing the $N \times N$ global system solver in favor of Cramer's rule, Standard Galerkin projection delivers a **~30x speedup** on the transient equations solve compared to direct Full Order Model (FOM) solvers.

The Non-Affine Assembly Bottleneck:

Even though the transient coordinate solver is 30x faster, standard Galerkin ROM still requires evaluating the warped gradients and Jacobian pullbacks over the entire mesh online. Because our notch profile is non-affine, we cannot separate parameters and space using offline matrices: a standard affine decomposition ($\mathbf{K}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \sum \theta_q \mathbf{K}_q$) is algebraically impossible. To eliminate this assembly bottleneck, **ECSW hyper-reduction is mathematically mandatory**.

Step 3: ECSW Integration (Energy-Conserving Sampling & Weighting)

The Energy-Conserving Sampling and Weighting (ECSW) hyper-reduction method reduces the online assembly cost by integrating only over a sparse subset of elements $\widetilde{E} \subset E$. The sparse weights $\mathbf{w}$ are trained to solve the Non-Negative Least Squares (NNLS) system:

$$\min_{\mathbf{w} \geq \mathbf{0}} \left\| \mathbf{b} - \sum_{e \in E} w_e \mathbf{G}_e \right\|_2$$

Swapping projection and summation, we assemble the reduced stiffness directly in microseconds: $$\mathbf{K}_{r, \text{ECSW}}(\mathbf{\mu}) \approx \sum_{e \in \widetilde{E}} w_e \mathbf{K}_{r, e}(\mathbf{\mu}) \quad \text{where} \quad \mathbf{K}_{r, e}(\mathbf{\mu}) = \mathbf{\Phi}_e^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{K}_{e}(\mathbf{\mu}) \mathbf{\Phi}_e$$

ECSW on Non-Mapped Domain (Why it Fails)

If elements are selected on a morphing physical mesh, the active elements shift in physical space. A static subset of weights $w_e$ trained offline becomes geometrically invalid under new parameters $\mathbf{\mu}$, causing high projection errors and solver instability.

ECSW on Mapped Domain

By pulling the equations back to the static reference domain $\hat{\Omega}$, the grid is permanently fixed. The ECSW training selects a single, static reference element subset $\widetilde{E}$ that remains optimal across all geometric parameter changes.

Side Note: Mathematical Retrospective on Phase 5.1 & 5.2 (Direct Physical-Domain Hyper-reduction)

In Phase 5.1 (Direct Coordinate Morphing) and 5.2 (Direct Physical ECSW), model reduction was formulated directly on the morphing physical domain $\Omega(\mathbf{\mu})$. Because control points and integration limits shifted dynamically, the sparse integration rules trained offline completely missed stress concentration fields as they moved, causing high numerical errors and structural singularities. Phase 5.3 resolves this completely by pulling the entire formulation back to the static reference domain $\hat{\Omega}$.

Algorithm: Reference-Mapped ECSW Training
1. Assemble Snapshots: Collect $S$ training displacement vectors on reference domain.
2. Construct G-Matrix: Assemble the virtual work representation for each reference element $e$:
    $\mathbf{G}_{s,e} = \text{vec}\left( \mathbf{\Phi}_e^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{f}_{int,e}^{\text{ref}}(u_s) \right)$
3. Assemble Target: Compute full reference domain force: $\mathbf{b}_s = \text{vec}\left( \mathbf{\Phi}^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{f}_{int,full}^{\text{ref}}(u_s) \right)$
4. Solve NNLS: Solve the constrained optimization: $\min_{\mathbf{w} \geq 0} \|\mathbf{G}\mathbf{w} - \mathbf{b}\|_2$
5. Sparsify: Select the indices $\widetilde{E} = \{e \in E \mid w_e > 0\}$ and store reference integration weights.

Dynamic Basis Projection Continuity (Parametric Sweeps)

A core challenge of using parametric ROMs is ensuring physical state continuity during shape transformations (parametric sweeps). When a geometric notch parameters ($\mu$ or $r$) are updated mid-simulation, the structural domain $\Omega(\mu, r)$ changes, requiring a recomputation of the Reduced Basis: $$\mathbf{\Phi}_{\text{old}} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \mathbf{\Phi}_{\text{new}}$$

The Discontinuity Jump Problem:

Directly applying the old coordinate amplitudes $\mathbf{q} = [q_1, q_2]^{\mathrm{T}}$ to the new basis results in a sudden, non-physical jump in the reconstructed spatial displacement field: $$\mathbf{u}(t) = \mathbf{\Phi}_{\text{old}} \mathbf{q} \quad \neq \quad \mathbf{\Phi}_{\text{new}} \mathbf{q}$$ This mismatch violates physical state continuity and instantly introduces numerical singularities, causing the dynamic solver to crash.

To resolve this, Phase 5.3 enforces $C^0$ physical continuity by performing a transient state projection. The existing high-dimensional displacement $\mathbf{u}_{\text{phys}}(t)$ and velocity $\mathbf{v}_{\text{phys}}(t)$ fields are projected onto the newly computed orthonormal basis functions $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{i, \text{new}}$: $$q_{i, \text{new}}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^N \phi_{i, k, \text{new}} u_{\text{phys}, k}(t), \quad \dot{q}_{i, \text{new}}(t) = \sum_{k=1}^N \phi_{i, k, \text{new}} v_{\text{phys}, k}(t)$$ This maps the transient states dynamically across the parameter-dependent manifolds, securing perfect physical continuity and absolute numerical stability during arbitrary real-time sweeps.

Quantities of Interest (QOI) & Von Mises Stress Pullback Warp

Extracting physical stresses is highly non-trivial because stress depends directly on spatial gradients. Under mapping pullbacks, these gradients are geometrically warped by the parameter-dependent inverse transpose Jacobian $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}$ and the determinant $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})$.

$$\epsilon_{xx}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{a=1}^{N} \left( \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{x}} + \frac{2\hat{y} d'(\hat{x}; \mathbf{\mu})}{H \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{x, a}$$ $$\epsilon_{yy}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{a=1}^{N} \left( \frac{1}{\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{y, a}$$ $$2\epsilon_{xy}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{a=1}^{N} \left[ \left( \frac{1}{\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{x, a} + \left( \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{x}} + \frac{2\hat{y} d'(\hat{x}; \mathbf{\mu})}{H \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{y, a} \right]$$

Hooke's Plane Stress constitutive relation evaluates the stresses, which are then used to reconstruct the equivalent Von Mises Stress $\sigma_{vM}$: $$\sigma_{vM}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sqrt{\sigma_{xx}^2 - \sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} + \sigma_{yy}^2 + 3\sigma_{xy}^2}$$

Phase 5.3b Extension: Geometrical Non-Linearity & Newton-Raphson

Phase 5.3b extends the reference-mapped ECSW hyper-reduction from linear to geometrically non-linear structural dynamics. Large displacements are modeled using the Green-Lagrange strain tensor pulled back to the reference configuration. Due to the non-linear stiffness relation, a Newton-Raphson iterative scheme is executed at each time step. The tangent stiffness matrix and internal force vectors are hyper-reduced by integrating only over the trained ECSW sparse elements subset, preserving the $\mathcal{O}(N_{\text{active}})$ online assembly cost.

Three Pillars of Mathematical Justification

Structural Guarantees:

  1. Pillar 1: Hilbert Space Homogeneity: Pullback coordinate mappings project varying physical manifolds $\mathcal{H}(\Omega(\mathbf{\mu}))$ onto a unified Sobolev Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}(\hat{\Omega}) = H^1_0(\hat{\Omega})$ defined on the static reference domain.
  2. Pillar 2: Mathematical Offline/Online Splitting: The parameter $\mathbf{\mu}$ is isolated from the spatial differential operators ($\nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$) and placed entirely inside the pullback Jacobian tensor $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}(\mathbf{\mu})$, allowing spatial elements to remain fixed.
  3. Pillar 3: Lax-Milgram Coercivity & Stability: Bijective diffeomorphism guarantees that the geometric transformation preserves coercivity ($a(\hat{v}, \hat{v}; \mathbf{\mu}) \geq \alpha \|\hat{v}\|^2$), ensuring numerical stability.

Critical Failure Modes:

• $\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}$ is the transpose of the inverse Jacobian matrix of the coordinate mapping.

The explicit expression for $\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}$ is:

$$\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \frac{2\hat{y} d'(\hat{x})}{H \left(1 - \frac{2d(\hat{x})}{H}\right)} \\ 0 & \frac{1}{1 - \frac{2d(\hat{x})}{H}} \end{bmatrix}$$

Where:
• $d'(\hat{x})$ is the derivative of the Gaussian displacement function, mathematically expressed as:
$$d'(\hat{x}) = -\frac{(\hat{x} - \mu)}{\sigma^2} d(\hat{x})$$

Applying this multiplication yields the physical shape function gradients:

$$\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial \hat{R}_i}{\partial \hat{x}} + \frac{2\hat{y} d'(\hat{x})}{H \det(\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_i}{\partial \hat{y}}, \quad \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y} = \frac{1}{\det(\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_i}{\partial \hat{y}}$$

Where:
• $\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y}$ are the physical shape function derivatives.
• $\frac{\partial \hat{R}_i}{\partial \hat{x}}$ and $\frac{\partial \hat{R}_i}{\partial \hat{y}}$ are the reference shape function derivatives.
• $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}})$ is the Jacobian determinant.
• $d'(\hat{x})$ is the notch derivative.

4. The Stiffness Integrand and B-matrix (Voigt Notation)

The global stiffness matrix is integrated over the reference domain. The B-matrix represents strain-displacement relations using the computed physical gradients:

$$K_{ij}(\mu) = \int_{\hat{\Omega}} \mathbf{B}_i^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{D} \mathbf{B}_j \det(\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}}) \det(\mathbf{J}_{\text{ref}}) \, w_g \, t \, \mathrm{d}\hat{\Omega}$$

Where:
• $K_{ij}(\mu)$ is the global stiffness matrix entry.
• $\mathbf{B}_i$ is the strain-displacement B-matrix in Voigt notation for the $i$-th basis function.
• $\mathbf{D}$ is the material constitutive elasticity matrix.
• $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\boldsymbol{\Phi}})$ is the pullback mapping Jacobian determinant.
• $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\text{ref}})$ is the reference-to-parent domain Jacobian determinant.
• $w_g$ is the Gauss quadrature point integration weight.
• $t$ is the physical beam thickness.

The strain-displacement matrix $\mathbf{B}_i$ is defined as:

$$\mathbf{B}_i = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial x} & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y} & \frac{\partial R_i}{\partial x} \end{bmatrix}$$

Where:
• $\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial R_i}{\partial y}$ are the spatial derivatives of the shape functions in physical coordinates.

Source Code Implementation
// solver.js (Lines 244-274)
B_phys[bidx][0][0] = dRdx_phys; // ε_xx
B_phys[bidx][1][1] = dRdy_phys; // ε_yy
B_phys[bidx][2][0] = dRdy_phys; // γ_xy (row 2, col 0)
B_phys[bidx][2][1] = dRdx_phys; // γ_xy (row 2, col 1)

// Integration factor assembly
const factor = safeDetJphi * detJ_ref * weightFactor * this.thickness;

5. Plane Stress Constitutive Matrix $\mathbf{D}$

For isotropic linear elasticity under plane stress, the stress-strain relation is governed by:

$$\mathbf{D} = \frac{E}{1-\nu^2}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & \nu & 0 \\ \nu & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1-\nu}{2} \end{bmatrix}$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{D}$ is the plane stress constitutive elasticity tensor in matrix form.
• $E$ is Young's Modulus of the material.
• $\nu$ is the Poisson's Ratio of the material.

6. Implicit Newmark-$\beta$ Time Integration

Dynamic equations are solved using the implicit Newmark-$\beta$ scheme. The average acceleration rule ($\beta = 0.25, \gamma = 0.5$) provides unconditional stability:

$$\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{u}_n + \Delta t \mathbf{v}_n + \frac{\Delta t^2}{2} (1 - 2\beta) \mathbf{a}_n, \quad \mathbf{v}_{n+1}^{\text{pred}} = \mathbf{v}_n + \Delta t (1 - \gamma) \mathbf{a}_n$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{u}_n, \mathbf{v}_n, \mathbf{a}_n$ are displacement, velocity, and acceleration vectors at step $n$.
• $\mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{\text{pred}}, \mathbf{v}_{n+1}^{\text{pred}}$ are the predictor vectors.
• $\Delta t$ is the time step size.
• $\beta, \gamma$ are the integration parameters.

$$\mathbf{a}_{n+1} = \frac{\mathbf{u}_{n+1} - \mathbf{u}_{n+1}^{\text{pred}}}{\beta \Delta t^2}, \quad \mathbf{v}_{n+1} = \mathbf{v}_{n+1}^{\text{pred}} + \gamma \Delta t \mathbf{a}_{n+1}$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{a}_{n+1}, \mathbf{v}_{n+1}$ are the corrected acceleration and velocity vectors at step $n+1$.

Source Code Implementation
// solver.js (Lines 373-427)
const beta = 0.25;
const gamma = 0.5;

// Predictor step
uPred[i] = u[i] + this.dt * v[i] + (this.dt * this.dt) * (0.5 - beta) * a[i];
vPred[i] = v[i] + this.dt * (1.0 - gamma) * a[i];

// Corrector step (after solving for uNext)
aNext[i] = (uNext[i] - u[i] - this.dt * v[i]) / (beta * this.dt * this.dt) - ((1.0 - 2.0 * beta) / (2.0 * beta)) * a[i];
vNext[i] = vPred[i] + gamma * this.dt * aNext[i];

7. Rayleigh Damping

Rayleigh damping defines a viscous damping matrix as a linear combination of mass and stiffness matrices:

$$\mathbf{C} = \alpha_M \mathbf{M} + \beta_K \mathbf{K}$$

Where:
• $\mathbf{C}$ is the global damping matrix.
• $\mathbf{M}$ is the diagonal lumped mass matrix.
• $\mathbf{K}$ is the stiffness matrix.
• $\alpha_M, \beta_K$ are mass and stiffness damping factors.

Step 6: Quantities of Interest (QOI) & Von Mises Stress Pullback

In engineering structural simulations, computing displacement fields $\mathbf{u}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})$ is rarely the final goal. The ultimate Quantities of Interest (QOI) are the physical stress fields, specifically the **Von Mises equivalent stress** $\sigma_{vM}$, to evaluate structural yield safety.

While reconstructing physical displacement from the reference coordinate solve is straightforward ($\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{\Phi}\mathbf{q}$ or $\mathbf{u}_{\text{phys}} = \mathbf{u}_{\text{ref}}$), extracting physical stresses is highly non-trivial because stress depends directly on spatial gradients. Under mapping pullbacks, these gradients are geometrically warped by the parameter-dependent inverse transpose Jacobian $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}}$ and the determinant $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})$.

The Strain Pullback warp:

Physical strains are defined by derivatives in the morphed spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x}$. By pulling these back to the reference coordinate space $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, we find that the strain components explicitly involve parameter terms that warp the gradients: $$\boldsymbol{\epsilon} = \begin{bmatrix} \epsilon_{xx} \\ \epsilon_{yy} \\ 2\epsilon_{xy} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial u_x}{\partial x} \\ \frac{\partial u_y}{\partial y} \\ \frac{\partial u_x}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial u_y}{\partial x} \end{bmatrix}$$ Applying the pullback derivative operator $\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} R_i = \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}^{-\mathrm{T}} \nabla_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} \hat{R}_i$, the physical strains expand to:

$$\epsilon_{xx}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{a=1}^{N} \left( \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{x}} + \frac{2\hat{y} d'(\hat{x}; \mathbf{\mu})}{H \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{x, a}$$ $$\epsilon_{yy}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{a=1}^{N} \left( \frac{1}{\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{y, a}$$ $$2\epsilon_{xy}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sum_{a=1}^{N} \left[ \left( \frac{1}{\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{x, a} + \left( \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{x}} + \frac{2\hat{y} d'(\hat{x}; \mathbf{\mu})}{H \det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})} \frac{\partial \hat{R}_a}{\partial \hat{y}} \right) u_{y, a} \right]$$

Where:
• $u_{x, a}, u_{y, a}$ are the displacement degrees of freedom at control point $a$.
• $d'(\hat{x}; \mathbf{\mu}) = -\frac{(\hat{x} - \mu)}{\sigma^2} d(\hat{x})$ is the notch shape derivative.
• $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) = 1 - \frac{2d(\hat{x})}{H}$ is the mapping Jacobian determinant.

Constitutive Stress Reconstruction

Once the physical strain vector $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu})$ is computed at a reference coordinate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$, the stress tensor components in physical coordinates $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = [\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{yy}, \sigma_{xy}]^{\mathrm{T}}$ are evaluated using Hooke's Plane Stress constitutive relation:

$$\begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xx} \\ \sigma_{yy} \\ \sigma_{xy} \end{bmatrix} = \frac{E}{1-\nu^2} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & \nu & 0 \\ \nu & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \frac{1-\nu}{2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \epsilon_{xx}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) \\ \epsilon_{yy}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) \\ 2\epsilon_{xy}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) \end{bmatrix}$$

Reconstruction of the Von Mises QOI

Finally, the equivalent Von Mises Stress $\sigma_{vM}$ is calculated at reference points using these warped physical stress tensor entries:

$$\sigma_{vM}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}; \mathbf{\mu}) = \sqrt{\sigma_{xx}^2 - \sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} + \sigma_{yy}^2 + 3\sigma_{xy}^2}$$

High-Stress Concentration Mapping

Because the mapping pulls the Gaussian notch depth $r$ dynamically into the terms $d'(\hat{x})$ and $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}})$, the stress equation naturally scales and concentrates stress around the notch tip. At the bottom notch apex where $\hat{y} = -H/2$ and $\hat{x} = \mu$, the Jacobian determinant reduces to its minimum value $\det(\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{\Phi}}) = 1 - 2r/H$, which mathematically scales up local strains and captures stress concentration peaks with absolute precision.
